welcome to my second story

I didn’t expect to ever log back into this dead site. Honestly, I forgot the password twice before my fingers finally remembered the muscle memory of the night I walked out on Lucius. I saw the missed calls. Hundreds of them, before I finally snapped my SIM card in half and dropped it down a storm drain. For months, I thought escaping Lucius meant I had survived. I thought the worst part of my life was behind me. I was wrong. You don't walk away from a fire intact; you just carry the smoke in your lungs until it chokes you somewhere else. That’s how I met Ivan. If Lucius was a storm that demanded everything from me, Ivan was the quiet, suffocating fog that crept in when I had nothing left to give. We met in a half-empty laundromat at two in the morning—two ghosts sitting under flickering fluorescent lights, staring at spinning machines like they were the only things still moving in the world. Ivan didn't ask me to burn for him. He didn't ask me to save him, or hold him together, or be his light. Instead, he sat beside me in the dark and told me that the dark was all there ever was. With Lucius, I was exhausted from trying to keep someone alive. With Ivan, I realized I was already dead, and he was just helping me get comfortable in the grave. He has this quiet, terrifying way of seeing right through the hollow shell I became. When I told him I felt empty, he didn't try to fill the void. He just smiled—a cold, crooked thing—and said, "Good. Empty things can't be broken anymore." I thought Ivan was my sanctuary. I thought we were healing each other in our own broken way. It took me six months to realize the truth: Lucius burned me down to the wick, and Ivan is just collecting the ashes. He doesn't love me. He loves the fact that I’m ruined. He loves that I have no fight left, no boundaries, no light to blind him. Whenever I try to pull myself back together—whenever a tiny spark of who I used to be tries to flicker back to life—Ivan quietly blows it out. Not with anger, like Lucius. Just with a whisper. A reminder of how much it hurt to care. A reminder that nothing ever gets better. Last night, I caught my reflection in the black screen of my laptop while Ivan was asleep on the floor beside my bed. For a second, I actually missed Lucius. Not because Lucius was good for me—he wasn't—but because at least when I was with him, I could still feel something, even if it was pain. With Ivan, there is no pain. There is no warmth. There is no cold. There is just nothing. I used to think my username meant I was sacrificing myself for someone else. Now I know the real tragedy of a candle: once the flame goes out, all that’s left is a cold lump of wax, sitting in the dark, waiting to be thrown away. Ivan is stirring in his sleep. If he wakes up and sees the screen glowing, he’ll ask why I’m trying to talk to a world that already forgot me. And the terrifying part is, I don't think I'd even argue with him.
